Our community

A school rooted in community

Frederick Law Olmsted School brings middle and high school students together in a community built around curiosity, creativity, and care.

Named for the influential landscape architect Frederick Law Olmsted, our school celebrates thoughtful design, environmental awareness, and the power of learning together. Students are encouraged to find their strengths, take on new challenges, and contribute to the life of the school.

Our story

From a gifted program to a citywide school

The Olmsted school story began in 1979, when Buffalo Public Schools launched a pilot program for gifted and talented students at School 56. As the program grew, it expanded to School 64 and other sites around the city before consolidating at Schools 56 and 64.

Families and educators wanted students to continue in the Olmsted program through high school. Working together, they developed a plan for a 5–12 school. In September 2007, the middle school program moved into the former Kensington High School building at 319 Suffolk Street; Olmsted welcomed its first freshman class in 2008. The program became a citywide school focused on gifted education.

Today, Frederick Law Olmsted School #156 serves grades 5–12 at Kensington. The school honors Frederick Law Olmsted, the landscape architect whose vision helped shape Buffalo’s park system.
